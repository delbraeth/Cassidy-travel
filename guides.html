Accessibility guides

Every place's own accessibility guide, in one list.

Before I plan a trip, I read the venue's own accessibility page. Here they are for the places my clients go most, from Waynesville to Orlando, plus the cruise lines, resorts and airports I book. Each link goes to the official page.

Links checked October 2026. Policies change, so confirm before you go, or ask me to check for you (opens in a new tab).

Close to home

Under 90 minutes from Waynesville

Waynesville and Dayton

  • Ohio Renaissance Festival (opens in a new tab)A walk-through guide: parking, gravel paths and the route around the steepest hills, restrooms, loud show times, service animals.
  • Caesar Creek State Park (opens in a new tab)Ohio DNR park page. Accessibility details are thin, so call the park office about the adaptive trail wheelchair.
  • National Museum of the U.S. Air Force (opens in a new tab)Visitor information page. The museum has no separate accessibility page.
  • Dayton Aviation Heritage National Historical Park (opens in a new tab)Site-by-site access for the Wright brothers sites: parking, elevators, restrooms, captioned films.
  • Boonshoft Museum of Discovery (opens in a new tab)Wheelchair loans, sensory bags, Sensory Sundays, a quiet room and a universal changing station.
  • Carillon Historical Park (opens in a new tab)Wheelchair loans, parking, the accessible carousel and railroad, and which historic buildings have limits.

Cincinnati, Mason and Northern Kentucky

  • Kings Island (opens in a new tab)Links the park's Guest Accessibility Guide, with ride access, rentals and the IBCCES Accessibility Card program.
  • Great Wolf Lodge Mason (opens in a new tab)Accessible rooms, pool lifts, zero-depth entries, waterproof wheelchairs and a sensory guide.
  • Cincinnati Zoo & Botanical Garden (opens in a new tab)Zoo Access for All: wheelchair and scooter rental, sensory bags, calming rooms, adult changing tables, sensory map.
  • Cincinnati Museum Center (opens in a new tab)Wheelchair and scooter rental, quiet zones, sensory maps, ASL tours and assistive listening.
  • Cincinnati Art Museum (opens in a new tab)Wheelchair loans, a sensory cart, large-print and braille guides, ASL on request.
  • Newport Aquarium (opens in a new tab)The aquarium's accessibility page, including wheelchair loans and sensory bags.
  • Creation Museum (opens in a new tab)Wheelchair and scooter rental, sensory kits, a photo social story and ASL dates.
  • Ark Encounter (opens in a new tab)Ramps and elevators, wheelchair and scooter rental, an accessibility map, sensory kits, ASL and captioning.

Columbus

  • Columbus Zoo and Aquarium (opens in a new tab)Wheelchair and scooter rental, KultureCity sensory bags, Quiet Hours and an adult changing station.
  • Zoombezi Bay (opens in a new tab)Accessible routes, a rides and attractions guide, water wheelchairs and sensory bags.
  • COSI (opens in a new tab)Free sensory bags, wheelchair and scooter rental, a calming room and a sensory map.
  • Franklin Park Conservatory (opens in a new tab)Ramps, elevators, paved paths, free wheelchairs and an accessibility PDF.
  • National Veterans Memorial and Museum (opens in a new tab)The museum's accessibility page, including sensory supports and wheelchairs.

Two to three hours

Weekend drives

Sandusky

  • Kalahari Resorts Sandusky (opens in a new tab)The resort's sensory-friendly page: calming room and free sensory kits at the front desk.
  • Cedar Point (opens in a new tab)Links the park's Guest Accessibility Guide, with ride access, rentals, a quiet room and the IBCCES Accessibility Card.

Hocking Hills and southeast Ohio

  • Hocking Hills State Park (opens in a new tab)Ohio DNR park page. Its trail list marks Ash Cave Gorge as wheelchair accessible.
  • The Wilds (opens in a new tab)Wheelchair-accessible safari vehicles on request, a caregiver ticket, a sensory guide and accessible lodging.

Lexington and Louisville

  • Kentucky Horse Park (opens in a new tab)Free wheelchairs, an accessible horse-drawn trolley, museum elevators and the Sunflower program.
  • Kentucky Derby Museum (opens in a new tab)Free wheelchairs, accessible track tours, induction loop, captioning, ASL on request and a quiet space.
  • Louisville Zoo (opens in a new tab)KultureCity sensory page: free sensory bags, quiet areas and a social story.
  • Kentucky Kingdom (opens in a new tab)The park's accessibility page and rider requirements guide.

Indianapolis

  • The Children's Museum of Indianapolis (opens in a new tab)Free wheelchairs, an adult changing table, ASL and assistive listening, sensory maps and social narratives.
  • Indianapolis Zoo (opens in a new tab)Sensory-friendly days, a calming room, an accessibility map and advance accommodation requests.

Longer drives

Four hours and up

Santa Claus, Indiana

  • Holiday World & Splashin' Safari (opens in a new tab)Accessibility page and 2026 guide made with Easterseals: sensory ratings for every ride, the Ride Boarding Pass, a Calming Room and family restrooms.

Cleveland

  • Rock & Roll Hall of Fame (opens in a new tab)Mobility aids, ASL interpreters, human guides and sensory supports.
  • Cleveland Museum of Art (opens in a new tab)Step-free entrance, accessible parking, free wheelchairs and screen-reader support.
  • Great Lakes Science Center (opens in a new tab)Wheelchair-accessible building and dome theater, a sensory space and sensory-friendly Sundays.
  • Cleveland Metroparks Zoo (opens in a new tab)Mobility rentals, a sensory room, quiet zones and caregiver admission.

Chicago

  • Navy Pier (opens in a new tab)Wheelchair loans, an accessible Ferris wheel, elevators and level-by-level maps.
  • Shedd Aquarium (opens in a new tab)The aquarium's accessibility page: exhibits, seating, parking and sensory supports.
  • Field Museum (opens in a new tab)Wheelchairs, sensory bags, low-sensory spaces and an audio and tactile guide.
  • Griffin Museum of Science and Industry (opens in a new tab)Building access, sensory notes by exhibit and low-sensory mornings.
  • CTA (Chicago trains and buses) (opens in a new tab)Which stations are accessible, elevator alerts and paratransit.

Nashville

  • Visit Music City accessibility (opens in a new tab)The visitor bureau's accessibility page, with a list of businesses recognized for access.
  • Nashville accessibility itinerary (opens in a new tab)The visitor bureau's trip plan: accessible attractions, sensory-friendly options, dining and downtown hotels.
  • Grand Ole Opry (opens in a new tab)Free loaner wheelchairs, lifts to second-level seating, assistive listening, and interpreters with three weeks' notice.
  • Ryman Auditorium (opens in a new tab)Wheelchair seating on the main floor and balcony, free loaner wheelchairs, and wheelchair-accessible tours.
  • Country Music Hall of Fame and Museum (opens in a new tab)Courtesy wheelchairs and large-print guides. Power chairs and scooters can't ride the Studio B tour shuttle.
  • Wheelchair Travel: Nashville (opens in a new tab)An independent guide written by a wheelchair user: attractions, hotels and getting around.

Norris Lake

  • Norris Dam State Park (opens in a new tab)An accessible paddling launch, adult changing table, accessible shelters and lodging notes.
  • Accessible fishing dock, Campbell County (opens in a new tab)TWRA's announcement of the ADA-accessible dock at the Davis Cemetery Access Area.

Gatlinburg and Pigeon Forge

  • Dollywood (opens in a new tab)Mobility policy, wheelchair and scooter rental, the Ride Accessibility Center and a downloadable guide.
  • Dollywood's Splash Country (opens in a new tab)Water park access, water wheelchairs and a downloadable guide.
  • Great Smoky Mountains National Park (opens in a new tab)Accessible drives and facilities, the accessible trail, campsites and off-road wheelchairs.
  • Pigeon Forge accessibility hub (opens in a new tab)The visitor bureau's guide to mobility, hearing, vision and cognitive access around town.
  • Gatlinburg trolley (opens in a new tab)Lift-equipped trolleys and paratransit. Gatlinburg has no general accessibility page.
  • Ripley's Aquarium of the Smokies (opens in a new tab)Sensory-friendly nights and mornings, plus sensory bags and headphones.
  • Anakeesta (opens in a new tab)FAQ on which areas are wheelchair accessible and which aren't, and gondola size.

Myrtle Beach

  • North Myrtle Beach beach wheelchairs (opens in a new tab)Free beach wheelchairs to borrow from the city.
  • City of Myrtle Beach accessibility (opens in a new tab)Which beach accesses are fully accessible, parking for disability plates, and beach wheelchair rental vendors.
  • Visit Myrtle Beach accessible travel (opens in a new tab)The visitor bureau's guide to accessible lodging, attractions, beach access and sensory-friendly planning.
  • Ripley's Aquarium of Myrtle Beach (opens in a new tab)Sensory-friendly hours with adjusted lighting, no music and a quiet room.
  • Broadway at the Beach (opens in a new tab)Guest services, including the on-site wheelchair and scooter rental.
  • Brookgreen Gardens (opens in a new tab)Visitor information, including mobility devices in the gardens.

Orlando

  • Walt Disney World: guests with disabilities (opens in a new tab)Parking, wheelchair and scooter rental, attraction access, hotel accessibility and ASL schedules.
  • Walt Disney World: Disability Access Service (opens in a new tab)Who qualifies for DAS and how to register by video chat before your trip.
  • Universal Orlando accessibility (opens in a new tab)The Attractions Assistance Pass, rentals, quiet rooms and the rider accessibility guides.
  • SeaWorld Orlando (opens in a new tab)The Ride Accessibility Program, rentals, ASL and sensory resources.
  • LEGOLAND Florida (opens in a new tab)The HERO Access Pass, sensory rooms and guides, and Certified Autism Center status.
  • Kennedy Space Center Visitor Complex (opens in a new tab)Equipment rental, autism resources and accessible tour buses.
