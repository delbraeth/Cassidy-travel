Why a cruise works well in a wheelchair

You unpack once, your room travels with you, and dinner, shows and the pool are all an elevator ride away. For many wheelchair users, scooter users and families traveling with grandparents, it's the easiest way to see several places in one trip.

It only works if the details are right. "Accessible" can mean a roll-in shower and room to turn, or just a few grab bars. That's the part I handle.

What I check before you book

  • The cabin. A fully accessible cabin has a roll-in shower, wider doors and room to turn. An "ambulatory" cabin has grab bars but isn't built for a wheelchair. I confirm which one you're getting, plus bed height, the balcony threshold and the distance to the elevators.
  • How few there are. Most ships have only a small number of fully accessible cabins, and they sell out first. I'll help you book early.
  • Every port. Some ports use small boats called tenders to get ashore, which can be hard or impossible in a wheelchair. I check whether your ship docks or tenders at each stop before I quote.
  • Shore excursions. Many tours are not wheelchair friendly. I find the ones that are, or arrange accessible private tours.
  • Equipment. Need a scooter, wheelchair, oxygen or a patient lift? It can be delivered right to your cabin. I'm a Certified Accessible Travel Advocate with Special Needs Group, the company most cruise lines use for this.
  • Your own chair. I share your chair's size and weight with the cruise line ahead of time so it fits the cabin door and the gangway.

Drive to the ship and skip the flight

Flying with a power wheelchair means trusting an airline with it. From southwest Ohio you can drive to a cruise port instead: Baltimore is about 7.5 hours, Charleston about 9.5, and the New York area about 10. Carnival, Royal Caribbean, Norwegian and MSC all sail from these ports.

Which cruise lines suit wheelchair users

  • Royal Caribbean, Celebrity, Princess, Norwegian, Holland America and Disney have accessible cabins in several categories on most ships, and wide public spaces.
  • Viking ocean ships have only about two fully accessible cabins each, and Viking river ships aren't a good fit for full-time wheelchair users.
  • River cruises in general are harder: small ships, steep gangways and docks where you cross other ships to get ashore.
