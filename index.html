Southwest Ohio favorites

Where Waynesville families keep going back

Four trips I plan most often, with how to get there, when to go, and what to know before you book.

Myrtle Beach

South Carolina's Grand Strand

Getting there
About 9½ hours by car, or a short nonstop: Allegiant flies from Cincinnati (CVG), with seasonal nonstops from Dayton (DAY) and Indianapolis (IND).
When to go
Late May, early June and September: warm water without the midsummer crowds. Ohio spring break weeks book up early.
Good to know
Condo resorts with kitchens and lazy rivers work well for three generations. Myrtle Beach approved free beach wheelchairs in 2026, and North Myrtle Beach has a ramp, mat and loaner chairs at 46th Avenue South. I confirm what's running before you go.

Norris Lake

East Tennessee

Getting there
About 4 hours by car, an easy weekend.
When to go
June through August for houseboats and swimming. September is quieter and the water is still warm.
Good to know
Big lake houses suit extended families, and summer weekends fill by spring. Many houseboats board by ladder or a steep ramp, so I ask for photos first. A fully accessible fishing dock opened in 2026 at the Davis Cemetery Access Area in Campbell County.

Gatlinburg & Pigeon Forge

Tennessee's Smoky Mountains

Getting there
About 5 hours by car.
When to go
October for fall color. From early November to mid-February, Winterfest lights up Gatlinburg, Pigeon Forge and Sevierville, and Dollywood runs its Christmas season.
Good to know
Many cabins have steps or steep drives, so I ask for photos of the entry, bathroom and parking. Dollywood publishes accessibility guides, and its DreamMore and HeartSong resorts have accessible rooms, some with roll-in showers. In the national park, the paved half-mile Sugarlands Valley Nature Trail is the accessible trail, and the park lends an all-terrain wheelchair.

Disney World & Orlando

Central Florida

Getting there
About 13 hours by car, or about 2 hours by air: Allegiant flies Dayton to Orlando Sanford, and Cincinnati, Columbus and Indianapolis have nonstops to Orlando International.
When to go
Weeks without school breaks are calmer, with shorter lines for everyone.
Good to know
Disney's Disability Access Service is for guests who, because of a developmental disability such as autism, can't wait in a regular line; register by video chat up to 60 days ahead. DAS isn't for mobility needs: if walking is the hard part, rent a wheelchair or scooter and stay at a resort on the monorail or Skyliner. Universal uses the IBCCES Accessibility Card; register 30 days to 48 hours before your visit.
